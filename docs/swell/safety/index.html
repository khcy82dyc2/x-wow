Safety

The chemistry is the dangerous part

Wet work only in a proper lab with a fume hood. Nothing chemical at home.

Several reagents are carcinogens, mutagens or reproductive toxicants (CMR substances). Not in a kitchen, a garage or a spare room. Follow the host lab's induction and risk assessments. Each chemical's hazard below is read from a named supplier's safety data sheet (SDS), cited in its row and listed under the table; raw chicken has no sheet. Suppliers' sheets differ and change, so check the one for the product you use. This is a starting list, not a risk assessment.

The law (UK, as the project's notes read it)

UK REACH Annex XVII (entries 28–30) means CMR substances can't be supplied to the general public. This covers acrylamide and, by Reg. (EU) 2018/675 as retained in UK law, formaldehyde. This law doesn't restrict the instrument itself, but the chemistry needs professional-user access, and it belongs only in an equipped, supervised laboratory working under its own safety rules. Never misrepresent yourself to a supplier.

Hazardous waste goes through a licensed route with records; nothing hazardous goes down the drain or into household bins. Research use only: no diagnostic claims, no human material, no live animals. This is a summary, not legal advice; check the current rules where you are.

Never bypassed, in code or in design

  • Lid interlock: opening the lid cuts the excitation LED and stops every motor (the steppers and the pumps) in hardware.
  • Moving parts: the stepper gears sit under fixed printed covers and the pumps' roller heads are enclosed, so fingers and hair can't be caught.
  • Leak cutoff: a leak trip cuts the pumps, any heater and the LED through a hardware latch that firmware can't clear.
  • Thermal cutoff: any heater gets a hardware cutoff rated ≤ 50 °C near printed parts. Version 1 has no heater on the stage at all.
  • No override: software never overrides these; there are no "force" flags.
  • Low voltage: ≤ 24 V DC inside, from certified external supplies with fused rails. Never wire mains.
  • Liquids: only the sealed sample vessel sits over the objective, with a drip ring, a sleeve and a leak sensor; no reservoirs above the optics or electronics.
  • Stage rules: no CMR liquids, no gel casting and no 95 °C steps on the stage.
  • Light: the excitation light, an LED behind a diffuser, must be in IEC 62471's Exempt or Risk Group 1 ("low risk") group as fitted, by its maker's rating or a measurement: X-WOW's rule is no light bright enough to hurt eyes looked at directly. A bare high-power blue LED can be Risk Group 2: the ams-OSRAM application note AN002 (2025-09-16, read 2026-10-12) gives a 470 nm blue LED of 5 cd as Risk Group 2. The light runs only inside the closed box, and opening the lid cuts it. Never look into the excitation light. The white and oblique LEDs, which the interlock doesn't cover, must be in IEC 62471's Exempt group, by their makers' rating. No lasers and no ultraviolet source in the reference build. The laser pointer in the family experiment is separate from the instrument and has its own safety box. Open: no LED is chosen yet, and whether a light this low-risk is bright enough for SWELL's fluorescence imaging hasn't been worked out.

  • This table was first written from the project's notes.
