SWELL

An automated expansion microscope, designed to be cheap.
Swell a sample a few times bigger, image it with modest optics, and let the muscle's own stripes check the result.

What you can do today: measure chicken with a laser pointer (a family experiment, with an adult), run the simulations and the ruler software on a computer (the tool, the download), and read every design file. What you can't yet: build the instrument. Its CAD, its wet-lab protocol and its instrument software aren't written.

Invention #1 · what it is

Make the sample bigger, then look with modest glass

SWELL is a design for an automated expansion microscope, meant to be cheap; whether it is waits until every part is priced. A small piece of supermarket chicken breast is anchored inside a gel that swells in water, so the muscle is physically enlarged, first by about 4–4.8× and later by 10×. A small 3D-printed microscope then scans the swollen gel, tile by tile.

01

Anchor and swell

The muscle's proteins are tied into a sodium-acrylate gel. Heat and detergent loosen the tissue, then the gel swells in water and pulls the proteins apart, ideally by the same amount in every direction.

02

Image

A printed flexure stage moves the gel over an objective and a mono camera. Hundreds of tiles and several focus planes are stitched into one image of the whole gel.

03

Let the meat check itself

Muscle comes with rulers. Each myofibril is a chain of sarcomeres, about 1.69 µm long in broiler chicken breast, by one published study (SWELL assumes supermarket breast is similar). Comparing the stripe spacing of each long myofibril before and after expansion would show how much that myofibril lengthened. Whether that equals the gel's expansion there is untested: dense protein structures can expand less than the gel around them (Damstra et al. 2022; Sheard 2021). Put together, these would give the strain map: a picture of how evenly the fibrils expanded across the gel.

North star

Chicken in, refill the chemicals, press one button. Out come images, a QC report and a draft paper, with no claim bigger than the QC supports.

Limits

What SWELL is not, yet

  • never built Never built. It stopped at the desk stage, before its first gate (G0): nothing has been bought, built or run on a bench. Exists: the optics choice, a parts list, the measuring method and a simulation that tests it on synthetic gels. Not yet written: the wet-lab protocol, the safety pack, the mechanical and fluidic design files and the instrument software.
  • simulation only Simulation only. Every result is computed on synthetic gels. On the red team's gel S3 the across-fibre ruler read widths too small on average (−17.8%), and only 28% of true pairs got an along-fibre ratio (what failed and why).
  • The ruler itself is untested. Whether a myofibril lengthens by the same factor as the gel around it is unknown; dense protein structures can expand less (the science).
  • Checked by AIs only. Every check so far is an AI review (separate Claude instances that didn't write the work), not peer review; no person has checked it (claims ledger).
  • Cost unknown. Only the priced lines are added up; most parts aren't priced yet (parts and cost).
  • Free to take, but not free of patents. X-WOW claims nothing, but can't promise that nobody else holds a patent; expansion microscopy itself is patented by others (patent notes).
  • Lab only. The chemistry is theory for equipped, supervised labs, never for home (safety and lab).

11 October 2026: who builds the instrument, its hot tools, its prices, and who wrote this
  • Who builds it. The law panel now says the instrument, like its chemistry, is for building in an equipped workshop or lab, by adults. Before, it said only that making a build safe and legal is the builder's job.
  • No hot tools. The parts list named a 3D printer and a soldering iron, both hot enough to burn, and a 12 V regulator whose maker says it can get hot enough to burn. They are designed out: a print service prints the parts, every join the builder makes is a screw or lever terminal or a ready-made plug-in lead, and the regulator is now an enclosed converter with screw terminals, mounted out of reach in a closed compartment. The parts list and the safety ratings changed with it.
  • The totals. This page said the core would cost £524–£784 and the full build £727–£1,072, that the box was over its cost targets, and that the top of the build range broke the £1,000 ceiling. Most lines in those totals were typed estimates, not prices read from a source, so none of that was known. Now a line shows a price only when it was read on a supplier page, and each total adds up those lines alone and says so: ≥ £103 for the core (2 of 24 lines) and ≥ £214 for the full build (3 of 31 lines). No total is compared with the targets until every line has a price. The 40X objective's €109 came from a search result, not its maker's page, which we couldn't open, so it is now not yet read; the 20X objective's price, read on its shop page on 11 October, is £111 with VAT, where the list said $149–$150. Each total is rounded down, so it never claims more than the lines add up to.
  • "Cheap". The page called SWELL "a cheap, automated expansion microscope" that images with "cheap optics", and its title said "a cheap expansion microscope". With most parts not yet priced, that isn't known, so it now says SWELL is designed to be cheap, with modest optics, and that whether it is waits until every part is priced.
  • Who wrote it. The licence panel said "Much of this material was written with AI assistance". An AI wrote it, working from the owner's brief and orders, as the AI section says; the line now says so. The AI section's heading, "Made with an AI. Directed by a person.", now reads "Made by an AI, from a person's brief."
  • The page's description (what search engines and link previews show) said SWELL is "free for anyone to build". It now says it is free to take and that the instrument is for building in an equipped workshop or lab, by adults.
11 October 2026: the concept model's cards
  • How the stripes are seen before expansion. Three places said it as if it were known to work. "A ruler grown into the meat" said "The before-image needs no label, because oblique light or crossed polarisers show the stripes"; the polariser films' card said two crossed films "make the muscle's own stripes glow before any gel is made"; the transmitted light's card said "a ring of oblique LEDs gives cheap phase contrast, so unstained stripes show". The design picks four oblique LEDs (differential phase contrast) for the before-expansion image, a choice predicted from physics and untested, and keeps the film pair only as a cross-check, because crossed film goes dark on fibrils lying near 0° or 90° to its axes. All three now say so.
  • The safety parts. The concept model now shows simple stand-ins for the printed covers over the stepper motors' gears (the covers themselves aren't drawn yet), and its cards say that opening the lid stops every motor and that each pump's roller head sits under its maker's cover, as the 10 October note below describes.
10 October 2026: what changed on this page, and why

Claims this page made in public, corrected. Nothing is changed silently; earlier versions stay in the site's public history on GitHub.

  • "Verified" became "cited". Results we read from other people's papers, pages and laws were tagged "verified". On X-WOW that word is kept for a result re-derived from the raw material by a named checker who didn't build it. These were read by an AI, so they are now tagged "cited", and the page says who read them.
  • Synthetic tests. "Synthetic data proves the analysis pipeline" now says synthetic data "tests the analysis pipeline against our own assumptions": a synthetic gel can't show how a real one behaves.
  • Gel S1b. "Gel S1b is the independent test" now says it was our own blind test, and not independent, because it came from our own generator. The harder test is red-team gel S3, built by a separate AI agent; it failed 3 of its 7 headline checks.
  • Withdrawn wording. "The method working, not failing", said of an error bar that went up, is deleted. "So the gate holds with margin" is withdrawn: red-team gel S3 showed that our blur model is not enough.
  • The family experiment. It now gives an age guide (8 and up); the adult does every step with the laser, the raw chicken, the knife and the glass; children mark the spots standing beside the card. Before, it named the adult only for the laser, gave no age guide, and left the knife, the raw chicken and the glass slides to no one in particular.
  • The download. The first version (2026-10-09) was withdrawn, because it carried the original brief's superseded plan to do the gel chemistry at home. The current version leaves that plan out: the chemistry belongs only in an equipped, supervised laboratory.
  • Host labs. The example shortlist of host labs and their prices is gone; the general checklist for finding a suitable lab, which sat beside it, stays.
  • Who may do the chemistry. The law panel said "So anyone can build the instrument, but the chemistry needs professional-user access: a lab, a community lab or a genuine business." Access to the chemicals isn't enough to make a place safe for them. It now says this law doesn't restrict the instrument itself, and that the chemistry needs professional-user access and belongs only in an equipped, supervised laboratory working under its own safety rules.
  • Words that implied more. "Naming a product means we used or specified it" and "The stage is a fork of the open OpenFlexure Microscope v7" now say the design specifies it and the stage would be a fork: nothing has been bought or built. "Proven on test images" and "no claim bigger than the QC proves" now say "tested" and "supports".
  • Requests to write in. The links that asked visitors to post on GitHub are gone. Questions and corrections go to info@x-wow.com.
  • A false line. "No earlier expansion microscopy on a 3D-printed microscope, or of isolated vertebrate myofibrils, was found" was wrong in its second half: a 2022 thesis, cited in the same row, expanded isolated rabbit myofibrils. The row now says so.
  • What a myofibril shows. "Shows, myofibril by myofibril, how much that spot swelled" now says that comparing a myofibril's stripes would show how much that myofibril lengthened, and that whether this equals the gel's expansion is untested.
  • The price of the first step. "The £5 laser-diffraction sarcomere experiment" and "Don't buy the cheapest unbranded pointer just to hit £5" used an undated estimate. A pointer whose shop page states Class 2 costs £17.83 with VAT (read 10 October 2026), and the page now says so; the line about cheap pointers is now a safety line.
  • Smaller corrections. The optics model's Strehl ratio is now called relative to the gel's surface, from index mismatch only. The Z-disc's width is now "30–140 nm, depending on the muscle and species", not "tens of nanometres". "CC0 1.0 Universal covers every document … in the SWELL G0 package" now lists what it doesn't cover: the official licence texts, quotations, and the X-WOW name and logo. The hero's "Free to build" now says "Free to take", because the instrument can't be built from these files yet. The pointer list allowed "red (650 nm) or green (532 nm)" and now says red only for families, because cheap green pointers can leak invisible infrared. "Making sodium acrylate in house" now says "in a lab, in a fume hood only".
  • Who did the work. "The project owner set the direction and made the calls" overstated the owner's part. An AI designed, simulated and documented SWELL, working from the owner's brief and orders; the download's decision record says which choices came from the owner's orders.
  • Water beads. The parts list and the safety table listed water beads as practice gels. They are out of the design: the UK's product safety alert says they can block a child's gut, needing surgery, or choke them. Their one job, watching a gel swell before any chemistry, moves to the first real gel in a laboratory.
  • Moving parts and power. The parts list had no covers for the stepper motors' gears or the pumps' rollers, and no supply for the 12 V pumps. It now has printed gear covers and pumps with enclosed roller heads, opening the lid stops every motor as well as the LED, and a fused 12 V rail feeds the pumps.
  • The safety table. Its own dated note, in the Safety and lab page's history, lists every line that changed there.
