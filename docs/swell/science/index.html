Why it works

Three ideas to keep it cheap

The project's design rule: chemistry does the resolution work, so the optics can stay modest. More expansion with a modest lens is meant to stand in for expensive glass.

1 · Grow the sample, not the glass

A lens cannot separate two points closer than about 0.61·λ/NA. For the 0.65 NA objective at 520 nm that is 488 nm. Expansion moves the molecules apart before the light ever reaches the lens, and the project's rule of thumb is effective resolution ≈ optical resolution ÷ E. In original-specimen units that is about 108 nm at 4.5×, and 79 nm at 10× with the 0.40 NA objective.

The lens's diffraction limit does not change; the specimen gets bigger. These are paper numbers, and still not electron microscopy: you only see what you label, and the chemistry can make its own artefacts.

Who got there first: Zhang, Chang and colleagues paired expansion with a webcam mini-microscope in 2016 ("expansion mini-microscopy"), reaching, after expansion, a resolution comparable to a benchtop microscope's on unexpanded samples, at a hundredth to a thousandth of the price. SWELL builds on their idea.

2 · A ruler grown into the meat

E = SLpost ÷ SLpre

Sarcomere spacing is measured on the same myofibril before and after expansion. The project's notes call the period blur-invariant: blur softens each stripe but leaves the spacing alone, so the ratio survives modest optics. The before-image should need no label: differential phase contrast with four oblique LEDs is predicted to show the stripes (the project's choice after the physics check; untested), with crossed polarisers as a cross-check.

It replaced the textbook A-band ruler, which needs sarcomeres long enough to show an I-band: only about 13% of supermarket breast at 4×.

Who got there first: Chen, Tillberg and Boyden (2015), who introduced expansion microscopy, imaged the same cells before and after expansion and measured how far lengths moved (under 1% of the distance in cultured cells, 2–4% in brain slices). Sheard and colleagues (2019) used the periodicity of sarcomeres and the fixed width of Z-discs as intrinsic reporters of local gel expansion in heart cells, and Sheard's 2021 thesis found the sarcomere factor lower than the gel's. A 2022 thesis by Migliore expanded isolated rabbit myofibrils, imaged them without labels and used the A-band as the ruler. VISTA (Miao, Lin, Qian and Wei, 2022) measured expansion by comparing label-free images of the same structures before and after. In our dated searches (docs/design/prior_art.md in the download, 10 October 2026) we found nothing that pairs each myofibril's label-free period before expansion with its own period after.

3 · Modest glass, just enough of it

Motic PL 40X/0.65 and PL 20X/0.40 plan objectives, one 100 mm achromat 25 mm behind the objective, and a mono IMX296 global-shutter camera (1456 × 1088 px, 3.45 µm) at £49.87. Together: 17.8× and 0.194 µm pixels, which is 1.03× Nyquist for the 4× track and 0.84× for the 10× track.

The stock OpenFlexure lens would undersample (0.66× Nyquist), and a colour sensor wastes signal, so neither is used. The stage would be a fork of the open OpenFlexure Microscope v7, run by a Raspberry Pi 5 and a Pico 2.

Built on open work: the OpenFlexure Microscope (Collins et al., 2020; build docs), expansion microscopy (Chen, Tillberg & Boyden, 2015), expansion mini-microscopy (Zhang, Chang et al., 2016), sarcomeres as intrinsic expansion reporters (Sheard et al., 2019), U-ExM (Gambarotto et al., 2019), pan-ExM (M'Saad & Bewersdorf, 2020) and TREx (Damstra et al., 2022). Every source, with what SWELL took from it, is listed under credits.

Words on this page

A short glossary

Sarcomere
One repeating unit of a muscle fibre, about 1.69 µm long in broiler chicken breast (one published study). A muscle contracts by shortening them.
Myofibril
A thin thread inside a muscle cell, made of sarcomeres joined end to end. SWELL uses them as rulers.
Z-disc
The thin line where one sarcomere meets the next.
Expansion factor (E)
How many times bigger the gel made the sample: a length after expansion divided by the same length before.
Eax and Etr
The expansion factor along a myofibril (axial) and across it (transverse).
Anisotropy
Growing more in one direction than another. On this page it is Eax ÷ Etr − 1.
Non-affine
A distortion that no single stretch, shift and turn of the whole gel can explain, such as a tear or a fold.
PSF
Point spread function: the blurred spot a microscope makes of a single point of light. The narrower it is, the finer the detail you can see.
Nyquist sampling
Taking at least two pixels across the finest detail you want to see. With fewer, that detail is lost.
Strehl ratio
How bright the peak of a focused spot is compared with a perfect lens's, from 0 to 1; 1 means no aberration. On this page it is computed in simulation, relative to the gel's surface.
q
The number the label-free stripe test hangs on: stripe contrast × √(photons per sample). The higher q is, the more precisely the stripe spacing can be read.
DPC
Differential phase contrast: lighting a clear specimen from one side, then the other, and comparing the two images, so thin transparent structures show up without a stain.
Clock tile
One patch of the gel imaged again every 50 tiles during a scan, so that slow swelling during the scan can be measured and taken out.
Overview camera
A small camera that photographs the whole gel, to measure its overall size before and after expansion.
CMR
Carcinogenic, mutagenic or toxic for reproduction: hazard classes some gel chemicals carry. UK law restricts selling them to the public.
G0
The first gate in SWELL's plan: a complete, checked design on paper, before any part is bought or any gel is made. SWELL hasn't passed it.
Simulated, synthetic
Made by a computer from a model, not measured on anything real.
