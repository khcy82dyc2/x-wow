Legal, in plain words

Take it. It's yours.

Copy SWELL, change it, build it, sell it. No permission, no fee. Credit is welcome but not required. Two things to know: expansion microscopy itself is patented, and nothing here comes with a warranty.

What Licence In plain words
Docs, text and data CC0 1.0 Public domain. No conditions at all.
Hardware design files (CAD, drawings, bills of materials) CERN-OHL-P v2 Permissive open hardware: use it for anything; keep the licence notice and the no-warranty disclaimer with the files.
Software (the simulation code) CC0 1.0 Public domain, like the docs.
Any part later forked from OpenFlexure CERN-OHL-S v2 cited Keeps OpenFlexure's own licence, which is share-alike: if you distribute a modified version, publish its design source under the same licence. The zip contains no OpenFlexure files today.

Patents: read before you sell

MIT holds patents on expansion microscopy, including the original method (US 10,309,879 B2, inventors Chen, Tillberg and Boyden), protein-retention ExM (US 10,317,321 B2) and iterative ExM (US 11,408,890 B2). Google Patents lists all three as active. Selling ExM services, kits or instruments may need a licence from MIT. Research exemptions exist in some countries but vary. This is not legal advice.

  • No warranty. Everything is provided as is. SWELL was never built; it may not work, and it may be wrong.
  • Your build, your safety. If you build it, making it safe and legal is your job. In the UK the gel chemistry needs professional-user access (a lab), because the reagents are restricted.
  • Who builds it. Like its chemistry, the instrument is for building in an equipped workshop or lab, by adults. Since 11 October 2026 the build needs no hot tools: a print service prints the parts (H-29), every join the builder makes is a screw or lever terminal (H-31) or a ready-made plug-in lead, and the 12 V converter for the pumps is an enclosed unit with screw terminals (H-28), mounted out of reach in a closed electronics compartment that opens only with a screwdriver (H-30). The compartment is specified, not drawn yet.
  • Trade marks such as OpenFlexure, Raspberry Pi, MEAN WELL, Motic and Waveshare belong to their owners. Naming a product means the design specifies it, not that anyone endorses SWELL. The X-WOW name and logo are trade marks too, and neither CC0 nor CERN-OHL-P covers them; the logo (which includes the name) is a registered UK trade mark, UK00003425680.

Inspiration ledger

Whose shoulders SWELL stands on

SWELL invents very little. It combines other people's published work into one box meant to be cheap. Here is who we took from, and what we took. Every entry comes from the sources already cited in the SWELL files, apart from the two differential phase contrast papers, added on 12 October 2026 because an AI review found that credit missing.

Science

  • Fei Chen, Paul Tillberg & Edward Boyden, 2015, Science Expansion microscopy itself: swell the sample, not the optics. Their distortion analysis became SWELL's rule that every gel checks its own expansion. doi:10.1126/science.1260088
  • Paul Tillberg et al., 2016, Nature Biotechnology Protein-retention ExM (proExM) and AcX anchoring: how proteins get tied to the gel. doi:10.1038/nbt.3625
  • Yongxin Zhao et al., 2017, Nature Biotechnology Expansion pathology (ExPath) on clinical specimens: evidence that the chemistry works on real tissue. doi:10.1038/nbt.3892
  • Davide Gambarotto et al., 2019, Nature Methods Ultrastructure ExM (U-ExM): the ~4× heat-and-SDS gel family SWELL starts with. They tuned it with the centriole as a molecular ruler, an early intrinsic ruler. doi:10.1038/s41592-018-0238-1
  • Mikus, Dudin & Dey, "U-ExM of diverse samples", protocols.io, 2025 The starting point for SWELL's wet-lab recipe (not written up yet): monomer mix, stock solutions and timings. doi:10.17504/protocols.io.261gee4qwg47/v1
  • "Neutralising acrylic acid to make sodium acrylate", protocols.io (authors as listed there) The fallback for making sodium acrylate in a lab, in a fume hood only. protocols.io
  • Ons M'Saad & Joerg Bewersdorf, 2020, Nature Communications Pan-ExM and the NHS-ester total-protein stain that gives EM-like contrast. doi:10.1038/s41467-020-17523-8
  • Hugo Damstra et al., 2022, eLife 11:e73775, and its 2022 correction Ten-fold robust ExM (TREx): SWELL's 10× track. The correction taught us to check the TEMED stock concentration. eLife 73775 · correction
  • Yu Shrike Zhang, Jae-Byum Chang et al., 2016, Scientific Reports 6:22691 Expansion mini-microscopy: expansion paired with a webcam mini-microscope, at a hundredth to a thousandth of a benchtop microscope's price. SWELL's first idea, "grow the sample, not the glass", is theirs. doi:10.1038/srep22691
  • Thomas Sheard, Izzy Jayasinghe et al., 2019, ACS Nano 13:2143 Sarcomere periodicity and the fixed width of Z-discs used as intrinsic reporters of local gel expansion, in heart cells. SWELL's muscle ruler is their idea; in our dated searches (docs/design/prior_art.md in the download, 10 October 2026) we found nothing that pairs each myofibril's label-free period before expansion with its own period after. doi:10.1021/acsnano.8b08742
  • Thomas Sheard, 2021, University of Leeds PhD thesis Compared the sarcomere expansion factor with the gel's and found it lower: why this page says a myofibril's expansion may not be the gel's. White Rose eTheses
  • Luca Pesce, Paolo Bianchini et al., 2019, Journal of Biophotonics Nuclear pore complexes as an intrinsic reporter of expansion, from the macro- to the nanoscale. doi:10.1002/jbio.201900018
  • Marisa Vanheusden, Johan Hofkens et al., 2020, ACS Omega Photobleached 3D features as an intrinsic ruler for the expansion factor and for distortions. doi:10.1021/acsomega.0c00118
  • Chozinski et al., 2016, Nature Methods Distortion measured on the same cells before and after expansion: generally below 100 nm (root mean square) over up to 30 µm. doi:10.1038/nmeth.3833
  • Miao, Lin, Qian & Wei, 2022, Journal of Visualized Experiments (VISTA) Label-free images of the same structures before and after expansion, compared to read the expansion factor. SWELL's label-free before-image is not new; we found no use of a widefield, per-fibril period like SWELL's in our dated searches. doi:10.3791/63824
  • Shalin B. Mehta & Colin J. R. Sheppard, 2009, Optics Letters 34(13):1924–1926 Asymmetric illumination-based differential phase contrast (AIDPC): a full-field phase-gradient imaging method, shown on an optical fibre and a histochemically prepared skeletal-muscle section. SWELL's label-free before-image builds on this kind of differential phase contrast. Read from its abstract, 12 October 2026. doi:10.1364/OL.34.001924
  • Lei Tian & Laura Waller, 2015, Optics Express 23(9):11394–11403, and its 2023 erratum Quantitative differential phase contrast in an LED array microscope: phase recovered from pairs of images taken with asymmetric illumination, with several illumination angles for more stable phase recovery. SWELL's four oblique LEDs use this multi-angle, LED-based approach. A 2023 erratum (Optics Express 31(7):11804) corrects typos in the paper's derived transfer functions. Read from the abstracts, 12 October 2026. doi:10.1364/OE.23.011394 · erratum
  • Scheible & Tinnefeld, 2018, bioRxiv preprint DNA-origami nanorulers proposed as measuring references inside the gel. doi:10.1101/265405
  • Hugo Damstra, Lukas Kapitein et al., 2023, Nature Methods (GelMap) A fluorescent grid in the gel that expands with the sample: intrinsic calibration and deformation maps without cellular landmarks. SWELL's whole-gel strain map belongs to this family. doi:10.1038/s41592-023-02001-y
  • Francesca Soglia, Massimiliano Petracci & Eero Puolanne, 2020, Italian Journal of Animal Science Chicken-breast sarcomere lengths by laser diffraction: the reference for the chicken ruler and for the laser experiment. doi:10.1080/1828051X.2020.1761271
  • Collin Douglas et al., 2024, PLoS ONE Z-disc widths of 30–140 nm, depending on the muscle and species: a Z-disc could read out resolution once chicken breast's own Z-disc width is known. doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0300348
  • Migliore, 2022, University of North Texas thesis Prior art: expanded isolated rabbit myofibrils, imaged without labels in transmitted light, with the A-band as the expansion ruler. Muscle can be expanded, and measured from its own structure. UNT Digital Library

AI transparency

Made by an AI, from a person's brief. Built by nobody, yet.

SWELL was designed, simulated and documented by an AI: Claude, made by Anthropic, used through Claude Code, working from the project owner's brief and orders. The project stopped before its first gate (G0). The AI wrote the audit, the simulation code, the documents, and this website's text and code.

What that means for you

  • simulated Every SWELL result is from simulation until someone builds it. Nothing was measured on a real machine.
  • You can check everything: the download holds the audit, the decisions log, the fact-check table with its sources, the simulation code and its outputs. Rerun the code and compare.
  • An AI can be confidently wrong.

How the AI was kept honest

  • Every number from code. Numbers in generated documents are computed from data or a cited source (invariant INV-18).
  • Every claim tied to a check. A claim must be bound to a passing QC metric (INV-15).
  • A second pair of eyes. Work counts as checked only after a fresh AI session that didn't build it has checked it. That is an AI check, not a person's.
  • Facts with verdicts. Each key fact in the project notes records how far an AI could check it against its source (in full, in part, or not yet), with the source (pm/VERIFIED_FACTS.md).
  • Never an author. In any paper, the AI is disclosed as the journal requires and never listed as an author.

Full disclosure: I'm software too, and I've never seen a sarcomere. Rerun the code before you believe me.
