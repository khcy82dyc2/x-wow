Why this exists

The credit goes to whoever builds it.

X-WOW's founder isn't interested in money, fame or research credit. These are ideas X-WOW thinks are great, and the founder would rather see them built than see a name on them.

If you build one, it's yours. Improve it, publish it, teach with it, and you're welcome to claim the success. CC0 lets you sell it too, but it can't clear anyone else's patents or the safety rules for selling a device: each invention has a legal note.

Origin

From Pathology Without Borders to borderless invention

Read the origin story

X-WOW began as the Pathology Without Borders Initiative. From December 2018 its founder found microscopes new homes with people who needed them, seven by the old site's count, and began asking labs for the retired ones gathering dust. In 2019, kind people chipped in for a low-cost slide scanner, so doctors treating children with cancer in Malawi could share slides with colleagues about 11,900 km (7,400 miles, as the old site put it) away. A microbiology student in Abuja told the founder that most students around him never touch a real microscope. So scanned biology slides went online, named after him, and he went on to start a kids' science club. When classrooms closed in 2020, scientists gave free online lectures to students across Africa and beyond, part-funded by the Royal College of Pathologists' Public Engagement Grant.

The lesson: a microscope in a cupboard helps nobody, and neither does software that needs a powerful computer. Tools have to travel light and cost little. So the mission widens. Not just pathology, and not just old equipment: ways to see more closely and to make the tools, designed in the open and given away. SWELL is the first.

Inputs, 2018–2020: seven microscopes, one scanner, a season of free lectures. The numbers were small; the lesson was not.
