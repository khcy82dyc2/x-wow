What it is

Make the sample bigger, then look with ordinary lenses

Instead of paying for better glass, SWELL makes the sample itself bigger. A small piece of supermarket chicken breast is anchored inside a gel that swells in water, enlarging the muscle about {{n:swell.e_a}} (later {{n:swell.e_b}}). A small 3D-printed microscope then scans the swollen gel, tile by tile.

01

Anchor and swell

The muscle's proteins are tied into the gel. The tissue is loosened, then the gel swells in water and pulls the proteins apart, ideally by the same amount in every direction.

02

Image

A printed stage moves the gel over a modest objective lens and a black-and-white camera. Hundreds of photos are stitched into one image of the whole gel.

03

Check with the muscle's own stripes

Muscle is striped at a regular spacing, about {{n:swell.sl_breast}} in chicken breast. Measuring the same stripes before and after swelling would show how much each fibre grew, and a map of that would show whether the gel swelled evenly.

The goal: put chicken in, refill the chemicals, press one button, and get images, a quality report and a draft paper that claims no more than the report supports.

What isn't known yet

  • It has never been built. Nothing has been bought or run on a bench. The mechanical design, the lab protocol and the instrument software aren't written yet.
  • Every result is a simulation, on computer-made gels, and some checks fail. Whether a muscle fibre grows by the same amount as the gel around it is untested.
  • Free to take, but not free of patents. X-WOW claims nothing, but expansion microscopy itself is patented by others (patent notes).
